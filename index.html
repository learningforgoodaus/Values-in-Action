Values in Action

Select a principle to learn more.

1. Safety First

Your number one priority is always safety. This includes:

  • The person's safety: Our response should never make the situation more dangerous for them.
  • Your safety: You must also be aware of your own safety.
  • The safety of others: This includes children, other staff, and customers.

2. Be Non-Judgemental

It is not our job to judge. It is our job to help. A person experiencing violence is often already feeling ashamed, scared, and blamed (by their abuser).

  • DON'T ask: "Why don't you just leave?" or "Why did you go back?"
  • DO say: "That sounds like a really difficult and scary situation."

3. Believe and Validate

One of the most powerful things you can do is believe someone. Many victims are told they are "crazy," "making it up," or "too sensitive."

  • Believe them. Take what they are saying seriously.
  • Validate their feelings. This means showing them their feelings are normal.

4. Respect Their Choices

It is the person's right to make their own decisions, even if we don't agree with them. We can provide support and information, but we cannot "rescue" them or force them to do anything.

  • Your Role: Offer support and options.
  • Their Role: Decide what (if anything) to do next.

5. Be Confidential

What someone tells you is private. You must not gossip or share this information with other staff, friends, or family.

  • Do: Treat the information with great care.
  • Don't: Leave notes lying around or talk about it in the staff room.
